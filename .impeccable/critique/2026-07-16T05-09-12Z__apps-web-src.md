## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Partial fail. The product is not generic end to end: privacy behavior, evidence visibility, scan review, and accessibility foundations are deliberate. The dashboard does use recognizable generated wellness-dashboard grammar: pastel radial gradients, a large identity lockup, repeated eyebrow labels, pill controls, and many similarly framed panels. The task routes are more credible than the first viewport.

**Deterministic scan:** 60 findings: 58 advisories and 2 warnings. There are 57 `design-system-color` advisories, one `design-system-font` warning, one `design-system-radius` advisory, and one `layout-transition` warning. Most color findings are literal alpha/shade derivatives in `apps/web/src/styles.css`; these indicate token drift but are not 57 distinct user-facing defects. The monospace fallback and 4px focus radius are likely false positives. The concrete warning is `transition: width` on `.query-bar-fill`.

**Visual overlays:** Mutable injection and the browser overlay succeeded. The overlay reported two anti-patterns: a single Aptos family and a width transition. The single-family finding is intentional under DESIGN.md and is therefore informational. A separate eyebrow/pill marker also appeared after reinjection, supporting the independent review's concern about repeated eyebrow scaffolding.

## Overall Impression

The app feels privacy-conscious, capable, and substantially more considered than a stock health dashboard. Its biggest opportunity is to make trust easier to consume: lead with profile, freshness, evidence quality, and the next useful action; move brand decoration and implementation detail into supporting roles.

## What's Working

1. Privacy is operationalized. The AI flow explains what leaves the device and what remains local near the consequential action.
2. Accessibility foundations are strong: native dialogs, visible focus, live regions, reduced-motion handling, minimum control sizing, and responsive table treatment are already present.
3. Evidence is inspectable. Timestamps, units, sources, limitations, scan review, and editable/read-only distinctions support informed review.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Missing goals are presented as personal facts

**Why it matters:** Substituting “Improve energy, sleep, and metabolic health” when no focus exists makes example copy look like stored health context. That damages the product's trust contract.

**Fix:** Show “No focus set” in read-only contexts. Keep the example only as placeholder/help text inside the editor.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Biological-age output foregrounds emotionally charged numbers before meaning

**Why it matters:** Chronological age, biological age, and acceleration read like equivalent facts while uncertainty and limitations arrive later. Users may interpret a model output as a health verdict.

**Fix:** Lead with model readiness and evidence quality; explain direction and uncertainty adjacent to the result; show missing inputs and limitations before or beside the headline number.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] AI answers receive more authority than their evidence warrants

**Why it matters:** “Answer” plus an unexplained percentage confidence creates scientific-looking precision. Evidence scope, time period, assumptions, and limitations are visually secondary.

**Fix:** Label the output as interpretation, place evidence scope directly above it, replace raw confidence percentages with calibrated language, and keep assumptions adjacent.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Dashboard hierarchy prioritizes identity and decoration over repeat-review value

**Why it matters:** The strongest first-viewport signal is the Vitara lockup, while freshness, changes, missing data, and next actions are distributed across equal-weight panels.

**Fix:** Make the first viewport answer: which profile is active, when data last changed, and what deserves attention. Reduce the brand mark to shell identity and vary surface treatment by importance.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Navigation and forms exceed the decision budget

**Why it matters:** Seven top-level destinations/actions and long unsectioned forms increase hesitation, especially for first-time users. ARIA tablists also need conventional arrow-key behavior.

**Fix:** Keep roughly five primary destinations, move configuration into a secondary menu, section long forms by user intent, and implement expected tab keyboard navigation.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex, experienced self-tracker:** The dashboard makes Alex pass a large brand block and scan several equal-weight panels before seeing what changed. There are no saved queries, multi-select correction, or obvious keyboard accelerators for repeated review.

**Jordan, first-time health-data user:** Jordan cannot immediately infer whether Import, Track, or Insights is the right starting point. Terms such as “observations,” “measurement code,” “age acceleration,” endpoint, and model require translation without a nearby glossary or explanation.

**Sam, keyboard and screen-reader user:** Native dialogs, focus states, labels, and live regions are strong. Remaining risks are tablists without documented arrow-key behavior, an icon-only Settings destination, and dense data tables that still need runtime verification at zoom and with a screen reader.

## Minor Observations

- “Vitara” and “Local Fitness Advisor” create an unresolved product-identity split.
- Repeated eyebrow labels have become layout scaffolding rather than useful hierarchy.
- CSV and scan imports offer inconsistent levels of pre-commit review.
- “Generate local insight” can mislead when a cloud-backed model is configured.
- Import success notices should offer a direct “Review imported entries” next action.
- Literal color variants should be promoted into semantic tokens instead of accumulating in component CSS.
- Replace the width animation on the query progress bar with a compositor-friendly state treatment or document why the layout animation is necessary.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the dashboard removed the Vitara hero, what single review decision should own the first viewport?
2. Should biological age be a headline metric, or a model output inside an evidence-first methodology view?
3. Does percentage confidence calibrate trust, or merely make generated interpretation look scientific?
4. Why should a person configuring a private health app need to understand endpoints and model identifiers?
5. What should happen immediately after import so the user can verify the right data reached the right profile?
