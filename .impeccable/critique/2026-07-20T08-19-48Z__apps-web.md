## Overall Impression

The product is strongest where it treats health information as evidence: imports preserve provenance, measurement detail retains units, timestamps, source, notes, and reference basis. The main opportunity is to make that evidence serve one concrete next step: a calm, prepared clinician conversation. The dashboard presently sells generalized wellness before it establishes trustworthy review work.

## What's Working

- The dashboard privacy and freshness strip communicates local storage, profile, recency, and import count without theatricality.
- Import review is accountable: source text, confidence, generated-code disclosure, row selection, and stale-mapping protection are visible in `apps/web/src/components/ImportDraftReview.tsx`.
- Measurement detail preserves source, note, timestamp, unit, reference basis, and editability rather than flattening everything into charts.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Wellness-branded dashboard conflicts with the private-evidence role

**Why it matters:** The floral mark, heart, “Thrive,” multicolor promise, and decorative dashboard washes make the app feel like a lifestyle tracker before users encounter reliable evidence. That weakens trust for clinician-conversation preparation.

**Fix:** Replace the hero lockup with a task-led heading, active profile, data freshness, and one action such as “Prepare a clinician conversation.” Remove the flower, heart, multicolor promise, and decorative washes from `DashboardPage.tsx` and its corresponding styles.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### [P1] The clinician-conversation outcome is missing

**Why it matters:** The product collects unusually good evidence but never turns readings, provenance, timing, concerns, and follow-ups into a usable handoff. Users leave with more information, not more readiness.

**Fix:** Add a preparation flow that lets users select evidence while preserving date, source, and range context; write questions and concerns; flag uncertainty; then export or print a compact clinician brief.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Status color implies medical severity without precise meaning

**Why it matters:** Green, amber, and danger treatments can read as healthy, concerning, and dangerous while representing distinct concepts such as a range comparison, model confidence, or data quality.

**Fix:** Use neutral labels for data quality and model confidence. Reserve warning color for explicit review-needed conditions and pair it with clear text such as “Outside selected reference range” or “Insufficient evidence.”

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Track conflates evidence reading with data administration

**Why it matters:** Personal range editing, manual entry, deletion, charts, history, and provenance share one hierarchy. A user preparing for a consultation has to navigate maintenance controls to read their evidence.

**Fix:** Make the default detail experience read-only. Move manual entry, personal-range policy, and destructive management into a secondary “Manage data” disclosure or route with clear scope and recovery language.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Upload mapping exposes too much technical detail too early

**Why it matters:** First-time importers meet mapping roles, diagnostics, units, codes, confidence, and large row sets before they know what requires attention.

**Fix:** Separate “Required to import” from “Optional provenance”; collapse optional mapping by default; show problematic or unrecognized rows first; place a one-sentence parsed summary above the editable table.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

**Anxious clinician-prep user:** Colored range labels and the Biological Age comparison can feel like a verdict. The disclaimers in `apps/web/src/pages/BiologicalAgePage.tsx` do not provide an emotional off-ramp or lead directly to useful clinician questions.

**First-time report importer:** `UploadImportFeature.tsx` and `ImportDraftReview.tsx` expose technical mapping and editable diagnostics before explaining what must be fixed, raising abandonment risk at the first high-stakes task.

## Minor Observations

- “AI-powered natural language query” should lead with evidence rather than technology: “Ask about your evidence.”
- Six top-level destinations plus settings and profile controls leave Care, Track, and Insights competing for interpretation.
- Keep Generated SQL in an advanced disclosure with a human-readable explanation.
- The empty care editor should recommend the next action rather than only requiring a selection.

## Questions to Consider

- What changes if the dashboard’s first action is “Prepare three questions for your next appointment” rather than “Generate insights”?
- What must a clinician trust first: the value, date, source, range basis, or the user’s concern? Why are those not one deliberate evidence object?
- Is Biological Age earning its emotional weight, or distracting from measurements and questions most likely to improve a real clinical conversation?
